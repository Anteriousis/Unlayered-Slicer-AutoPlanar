Multiplanar Printing

Unlayered Slicer: printing different parts of one object in different directions.

An ordinary slicer prints every layer flat, so anything that overhangs needs support. Multiplanar printing lets you choose a different build direction for each part of the object. An arm sticking out sideways can be printed along its own length, with no support under it.

You tell the slicer where to change direction with cut planes. A cut plane is a marker you place on the model that means: "divide the part here, and print everything beyond this plane in the direction the plane faces."

  • The part in your project is never actually cut. The planes are stored on the object; the pieces (called segments), their directions and their print order are worked out when you slice, and you can edit the planes at any time.
  • The segment sitting on the bed prints first, flat, like a normal print. Each other segment is then printed on top of the one below it, in its own direction.
  • On an Unlayered 5-axis printer the bed tilts and turns under the nozzle, so a segment can lean any way, even fully sideways like the arm above.
  • On an ordinary 3-axis printer (Printer type Virtual) the nozzle stays upright, so a segment can only lean as far as your hotend allows before it would hit the part: about 35–45° with the default hotend shape. The slicer refuses steeper ones.

1  Before you start

Two settings must be right before the Cut Planes tool will work.

Non-planar printing must be on for your printer

On an Unlayered 5-axis printer it is already on: skip this.

On any other printer, either:

  • click the greyed-out Cut Planes icon and answer Yes to Turn on non-planar printing (Unlayered Slicer 0.1.3 and later), or
  • open the printer settings (the edit icon next to the printer name in the left sidebar), go to the Motion ability tab, find the Non-planar Printing section and tick Enable non-planar printing. Then set Printer type to Virtual for an ordinary 3-axis printer. Save the printer preset to keep the change.

In Unlayered Slicer 0.1.2 and earlier these two settings are hidden in Simple mode: switch the Simple / Advanced / Expert selector at the top of the settings panel to Advanced to see them. From 0.1.3 they are shown in every mode.

Print sequence must be "By group"

Each segment is printed completely, one after another, so the job must print by group rather than layer by layer across the whole plate. The setting is at Process → Others → Special mode → Print sequence. You don't have to find it: when you add your first cut plane the slicer asks to switch it for you. Unlayered printer profiles already use By group.

2  Quick start

Try it on a simple L-shaped part like the one above: an upright post with an arm sticking out near the top.

On an ordinary 3-axis printer (Printer type Virtual) a fully sideways arm would need a 90° tilt, which an upright nozzle can't reach: the plane stops at the tilt limit. Practise instead on a branch that leans out at about 45–60°, and tilt its plane only as far as the panel allows (Max tilt this way).
  1. In the Prepare tab, click the object to select it.
  2. Click the Cut Planes icon in the toolbar along the top of the 3D view. If it is greyed out, non-planar printing is off for this printer (see Before you start), or no single object is selected.
  3. A plane appears through the object, with a panel of controls beside it. Move the plane to where the arm meets the post:
    • drag the arrow to slide the plane along the direction it faces, and drag the rings to tilt it; or
    • click Select face and then click a face of the model, such as the arm's flat end, to line the plane up with it.
    The plane should face along the arm, pointing away from the post: that is the direction the arm will be built in. Use Flip cut plane if it points the wrong way. The coloured preview shows the segments you would get.
  4. Click Add cut plane. The plane is saved in the list in the panel. The editing plane stays where it is, so you can move it and add another.
  5. When asked to switch the print sequence to By group, answer Yes.
  6. Click Slice. In the preview, the post prints flat first, then the arm prints in its own direction. You can add more planes for more segments: each plane starts a new one.
The panel's Tutorial button and Help → Multiplanar Tutorial open this page.

3  How segments are worked out

The planes divide the object into segments. The segment resting on the bed is the root and prints flat. Every other segment sits on exactly one plane, its base plane, and is printed in the direction that plane faces, on top of the segment on the other side of the plane. Arrows on each plane in the 3D view show that direction.

___________ | arm ⟋ | plane 2 → the arm builds in plane 2's direction |___⟋⟍______| | tower | plane 1 → the tower builds in plane 1's direction ___|___________|___ | root | printed flat on the bed, first |_________________|
  • Physical order always wins. A segment always prints after the segment it sits on: root first, then outward. Reordering planes cannot change that.
  • Sibling order follows your plane list, in reverse. When two segments sit on the same parent, the one from the first plane in the list prints last. Use the arrows in the plane list to reorder.
  • Build direction override. To print a segment in a different direction without moving its plane, select the plane and use the B/C angle fields in the panel. Reset direction makes it follow the plane again.

5  Pipe Cut

For a bent pipe, duct or tube, the Pipe Cut tool places the planes for you. It finds the centreline of the bore and cuts the part into short chunks along it, so each chunk prints square to the pipe. It is for hollow, tube-shaped parts only. A solid or boxy model (a Benchy, a bracket) gets nothing useful from it. Its icon sits next to Cut Planes in Advanced and Expert mode.

  1. Open the Pipe Cut tool. The centreline is found automatically; edit it if needed: move, delete, or connect nodes.
  2. Click Cut along pipe: one cut plane is added per centreline step (at the step midpoint, facing along the pipe), and you land in the Cut Planes tool with the full list ready to review and edit.
  3. Prefer manual control? Use Step to walk the proposed cuts one by one and Cut here to add only the planes you want.
The object is not split: Pipe Cut fills in the same editable plane list as the Cut Planes tool. At most 64 planes per object are supported.

6  Safety checks

  • Hotend collision check. After slicing, the hotend is modelled as a four-sided pyramid with its tip at the nozzle (by default 45° per side, reaching 40 mm from the tip), and the whole toolpath is checked against everything printed earlier. A collision stops the print with a message naming the segments and G-code lines. Re-orient the segment, add clearance, or set the pyramid to match your real hotend in the printer settings (Settings reference).
  • Tilt limit on a 3-axis printer. With Printer type Virtual, a plane cannot be tilted further than the hotend shape allows. The panel shows the limit, and the plane stops there when you drag it.
  • Thin segments. If any segment would be thinner than the first layer, the feature turns itself off for that object and the panel says why. This includes two nearly parallel planes leaving a sliver between them.
  • Duplicate planes (the same plane added twice, facing either way) are ignored; the first one wins.

8  Settings reference

Printer settings → Motion ability → Non-planar Printing

The rest of this section only appears once Enable non-planar printing is ticked. Unlayered printer profiles set all of it for you.

SettingWhat it does
Enable non-planar printingThe master switch. Off: every object slices in one piece and the Cut Planes and Pipe Cut tools are greyed out.
Printer typeWhich two rotary axes the machine has: A/B, B/C or A/C. Virtual is for an ordinary 3-axis printer: no rotary moves are written and the nozzle stays upright.
A / B / C axis limitsRotation limits of each rotary axis, stored with the machine definition.
Chunk departure distance, Chunk approach heightHow far the nozzle backs away from a finished segment, and how high above the next one it stops, when moving between segments.
Nozzle tip to B axis distanceFor real 5-axis machines: the arm length between the nozzle tip and the B rotation axis.
Hotend envelope radius, Hotend side angles (+X, −X, +Y, −Y)The hotend shape used by the collision check and the 3-axis tilt limit: a pyramid with these side angles, reaching this far from the tip. A radius of 0 switches the check off.
Gantry height above nozzle, Max tilt angle, Max print height (from the bed)Virtual printer type only: extra limits on how far a segment may lean or climb before the print would hit the machine. Max print height is a height measured from the bed, usually close to the printer's build height, not a clearance. 0 means not used.

Process → Others → Multiplanar

Layer Rotation B and Layer Rotation C tilt the layers of a whole object, without any cut planes: B rotates them about the Y axis, C about the Z axis. They only apply while non-planar printing is on.

Process → Speed → Multiplanar cut plane settings

The first layers of each segment are laid on the printed face of the segment below, not on the bed. These settings give those layers their own fan, speed and flow (Layers on printed material sets how many layers count). They are shown only while non-planar printing is on.
